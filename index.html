Perimeter

Our study replaces the square patches of a vision transformer with the regions of an oriented cell complex built for each image. Every border carries a sign. One signed pass over the borders returns each region's exact area, center and spread, so the model sees parts, not pixels.

Request the full study

Results

  • +1.8 points top-1 over plain patches on all three seeds, with 69% of the multiply-adds. Imagewoof at 128 px, CPU pilot.

  • Flat with resolution. 13.0 to 14.4 regions per image from 64 to 1,024 px, where patches grow with the square of the side.

  • Proved. The signed pass recovers exact area, centroid and second moments. Without signs, two different regions can give the same inputs.

  • Not yet shown. A gain from the signs themselves: 0.25 points, inside seed noise. On a CPU the pilot ran at 349 images/s against 704.

A conservative solver

Regions become control volumes, borders become faces, and the signed incidence matrix becomes the divergence. A flux that leaves one region enters its neighbor with the opposite sign, so energy balances to machine precision whatever the network has learned.

u

B

Light and heat

The physics we model spans three infrared regimes. Near-infrared light scatters and diffuses. Short-wave infrared is absorbed and attenuated. Long-wave infrared is heat: emission at a surface and conduction underneath. One solver couples radiative transfer with heat transport, so the three constrain a single model.

Regimes

  • NIR, 0.7 to 1.0 µm. Scattering dominates. Light diffuses far through a turbid medium before it is absorbed.

  • SWIR, 1.0 to 2.5 µm. Absorption bands of water and lipids shape the spectrum; attenuation carries composition.

  • LWIR, 8 to 14 µm. Thermal emission at the surface, set by conduction and sources below it.

  • Coupled. Absorbed light becomes a heat source. Radiative transfer and heat transport share one mesh and one receipt.

Built for GPUs

Cost follows tokens, and region tokens do not multiply as the picture sharpens. A few dozen regions and their borders map onto batched GPU kernels: the same work at 64 pixels as at 1,024, which is what keeps latency low and throughput high.

Measured

  • 37 tokens at most per image for the region models, class token included, against 65 for plain patches.

  • 69% of the multiply-adds of the patch baseline, at a higher score.

  • About 0.1 s for the reference solver above to solve 10,240 cells to a relative residual of 10⁻⁷ on one CPU thread. The inverse fit takes about a second.

  • Next: one H100. 89 queued jobs under a $100 cap, each decision rule written before its run. GPU throughput is measured there, not claimed here.

Problems it solves

The method fits any medium that light or heat moves through, wherever a field has to be computed fast, run backward, or trusted. These are problem classes, not markets.

Compatible with

  • Forward simulation. Fields from sources, materials and geometry, on demand, for sweeps that would take a classical solver days.

  • Inverse problems. Hidden sources and properties recovered from surface data, each with a 95% interval.

  • Uncertainty. Every answer ships with its residual, conservation checks and interval, and the rule it was judged by.

  • Synthetic data with ground truth. Fields and spectra where the answer is known exactly, to train and test other models.

  • Design of experiments. Thousands of cheap forward runs to choose the few expensive measurements worth making.
